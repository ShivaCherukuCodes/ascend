# Product requirements

## Product promise

Ascend helps a person make a better decision about their day. It brings health, fitness, nutrition, recovery, communication, career, habits, and long-term growth into one trusted, understandable experience.

## Primary user need

People already have data, plans, and good intentions. What they lack is a single companion that understands context and turns it into a small, encouraging next action.

## Information architecture

- **Today** — the helpful next step, daily plan, progress, and reflection.
- **Journey** — longer-term goals, milestones, and patterns.
- **Plans** — editable plans for the areas of life a person chooses to improve.
- **Inbox** — coach messages, reminders, and items that need attention.
- **Me** — preferences, context, privacy controls, and personal profile.

## First-release outcomes

1. A person can share enough context for Ascend to understand their priorities.
2. They receive a focused daily view with a clear explanation for each recommendation.
3. They can follow, adapt, or defer a plan without feeling they failed.
4. They can see meaningful progress over time.
5. They can understand and control what information influences guidance.

## Non-goals for the first release

- Replacing clinicians, therapists, financial advisers, or emergency services.
- Tracking every possible life metric.
- Maximising engagement through guilt, streak pressure, or opaque nudges.
- Treating AI output as unquestionable authority.

## Measures of success

The leading signal is voluntary daily return: would someone genuinely want to open Ascend each morning? Supporting signals include plan follow-through, helpfulness feedback, confidence in recommendations, and sustainable progress—not raw screen time.
